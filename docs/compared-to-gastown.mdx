[Gas Town](https://github.com/steveyegge/gastown) is Steve Yegge's orchestrator for running
many coding agents at once on one machine. Baton, with the built-in `pr` course (implement →
review → fix → merge, R-BTN-74), addresses the same problem: keeping a fleet of agents busy on
a backlog without two of them doing the same work, and keeping the work moving when an agent
crashes, stalls or fills its context. The two make different bets about who keeps order. This
page compares them, as [Baton compared with Temporal](compared-to-temporal.mdx) does for
durable execution. The rules referred to (`R-BTN-n`) are in `spec/BATON_SPEC.md`. What this
page says about Gas Town comes from [its README](https://github.com/steveyegge/gastown) and
published coverage — [heise](https://www.heise.de/en/background/Full-Control-Gas-Town-Orchestrates-Ten-or-More-Coding-Agents-11178824.html),
[The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/steve-yegges-ai-agent-orchestration-project-gas-town-comes-to-the-cloud-and-brings-the-wasteland-with-it/)
and [SD Times](https://sdtimes.com/softwaredev/gas-town-by-kilo-multi-agent-orchestrator-now-available/)
— as of October 2026; Gas Town is alpha software and changes quickly. On Baton's side, rules R-BTN-106 and
later (§2m: the runner's channel, retries per leg, the context refresh, the `epic` course,
decision severities and the rest) are a 2026-10-17 proposal, specified and not yet built; the
rest of this page describes them as the spec does.

## The one-line difference

- **Gas Town supervises agents with agents.** A coordinating agent (the Mayor) breaks work
  into issues and assigns them; per-project agents watch the workers (the Witness), merge their
  output (the Refinery) and patrol the whole town (the Deacon). Work state is kept in git
  (beads and hooks), and the agents run as tmux sessions on the host.
- **Baton is a lease server over git.** No agent coordinates. Workers pull the next leg they can
  do, hold it under a fenced lease, and report where the ticket got to; git holds every ticket
  fact, and Baton keeps only leases, the owners' limits and a record of what it saw (R-BTN-39,
  R-BTN-66). Workers can be on any machine or in the cloud, and a person can do any leg by
  pushing to git.

## What Gas Town gives that Baton does not

1. **A standing coordinator.** Tell the Mayor what to build and it decomposes the work,
   bundles it into convoys, dispatches it and keeps the whole picture as the work goes on.
   Baton's `epic` course plans too (R-BTN-124) — its `plan` leg files child tickets, and
   `replan` answers a withdrawn one — but each is a leg that ends; no agent watches the epic
   between legs or re-plans on its own initiative.
2. **Nudging live agents.** A Witness nudges a stuck agent. Baton notices silence and
   staleness and reaps a silent claim, and its runner refreshes a session whose context is
   filling under the same claim (R-BTN-123); but a live, stuck agent runs until its leg's
   deadline (R-BTN-90).
3. **A merge queue in the box.** The Refinery batches merge requests, runs the gates on the
   merged stack and bisects a red batch. Baton's `merge` leg merges one change at a time and
   leaves queuing to the forge.
4. **Steps inside a step.** A molecule tracks the steps of a formula, and a poured wisp
   recovers from a checkpoint. Baton's unit of durability is the station: a leg that dies is
   restarted from its hand-off document.
5. **Push dispatch.** `gt sling` sends a given bead to a given rig and runtime. Baton never
   assigns; an owner can expedite, hold or re-route a ticket (§8, R-BTN-83), not pin it to a
   worker.
6. **Talking to the previous session.** Seance lets an agent ask its predecessor. Baton keeps
   pointers to the transcript, the hand-off document and the session link; asking a
   predecessor is reserved for a course's own leg, to be built when a problem needs it.
7. **OpenTelemetry out of the box,** and a federation of towns (the Wasteland).

## What Baton gives that Gas Town does not

1. **One holder per ticket, enforced.** A claim is a fenced lease: two workers never hold one
   ticket, and a worker whose lease lapsed is refused at its next push (R-BTN-4, R-BTN-6). Gas
   Town's agents coordinate by convention and supervision.
2. **Any machine, any cloud.** Workers on laptops, VMs and cloud sessions share one Baton over
   signed HTTP (R-BTN-13, R-BTN-107). Gas Town's roles run as tmux sessions on one host.
3. **Git is the truth.** Everything a ticket is, is in git; a person or another tool moving it
   is simply seen by the next sync, and every disagreement is logged before git wins
   (R-BTN-85). Baton could be rebuilt from git and its exports.
4. **Limits agents cannot change.** Keys, grants, owner bars, required bars, independence, retry
   bounds and check conclusions are Baton state that only owners set (R-BTN-66, R-BTN-122). An
   agent that edits the course file cannot loosen them.
5. **Independent review by construction.** A course can bar the key that wrote a change from
   reviewing it (R-BTN-106), and merge checks come from keys only owners register (R-BTN-114).
6. **Courses are data, checked before they ship.** `baton test-course` checks a course change
   against live tickets, contracts and schemas (§2d, R-BTN-108); typed outputs, entry
   requirements and retry policies per leg (R-BTN-109, R-BTN-122) make a leg's interface
   explicit.
7. **People are first-class.** Decisions reach people through couriers and wait without a time
   limit; a person's answer in git moves the ticket (R-BTN-78, R-BTN-116).
8. **A checked core.** Baton's core is a Quint model, and every integration scenario's trace is
   replayed through it (R-BTN-68).

## Where each fits

Gas Town fits one developer driving many agents on one machine, interactively: a person
talking to a Mayor, watching the feed, and stepping in. It makes the most of one powerful host
and a large subscription.

Baton fits a team's backlog worked by a fleet that comes and goes — agents on several machines
and in the cloud, people, and other tools — where who may do what must hold against agents,
work must be reviewable in pull requests, and the record must survive any single machine.

They can be combined: a Gas Town polecat can be a Baton worker, claiming legs through `baton
run` and doing them with Gas Town's machinery inside the leg.
