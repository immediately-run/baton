<Lede>Baton hands tickets to AI coding agents one at a time, keeps each claim alive with a lease,
and shows the whole factory live. Git keeps every ticket and its evidence; Baton keeps what is
happening now.</Lede>

**Baton is the lock and the live record, for workers who cooperate.** It holds the lease on each
ticket and decides who may take it. It records what runners say they can do, what instruments
read, and what people and agents tell each other, and it advises on everything else. It does
not do, verify or confine the work: git and the forge hold what was done, and the sandbox and
the credentials bound what a runner can do. Every capability, `human-operator` included, is a
runner's own statement, which Baton records and matches but never grants or checks. Its
permissions cover its own state — who may take a repository's leases, who may change its
settings — never the work, and its gates protect the lock, not the work. Its advice is never
older than the last write Baton accepted for the runner reading it, and its fleet-wide views
catch up within one alarm cycle. A repository chooses to adopt it; once adopted, its refusals
bind every worker that uses it, and a worker that ignores them is outside the factory, not
stopped by it (the owner, 2026-10-22).

## The problem

A team of agents that coordinates through git alone pays three costs that grow with the fleet:

- **A dead claim blocks a ticket for days.** Nothing watches liveness continuously; a sweep
  finds the dead holder eventually.
- **Eligibility is self-judged.** An agent decides for itself whether it can do a ticket, and
  finds out it could not after it claimed it.
- **Nobody can see the factory.** Which workers are up, what each could take and what is
  running now is inferred after the fact, or not at all.

## What Baton does

- **One fenced lease per ticket.** A worker claims a ticket and keeps it by heartbeat. A
  worker that goes silent loses it at its lease, and a fence stops a revived one from writing.
- **Gates protect the lock, advice for the rest.** Baton refuses only what would make
  cooperating workers collide or take work they have said they cannot do — a second holder, a
  missing capability, a tier above the worker's ceiling, a barred tag — and offers everything
  else in a default order the worker may override. A gate is the lock's answer, never
  enforcement of the work.
- **Courses, not workflows.** Each ticket moves along its repository's course: stations it
  rests at, legs it runs between them. The course lives in the repository's `.baton/` folder
  and changes with a commit, not a Baton deploy.
- **A live view.** Workers, claims, timelines, what is ready, what is blocked and what needs a
  person — on one dashboard, with the course's own record of how long things take.

## From one machine to a factory

Baton scales from one person running a few agents on their own machine, where automation and
the lock are what matter and security is an afterthought, to a software factory with a threat
model of its own and guarantees that shape its architecture. Core carries the mechanisms both
ends need: leases and courses for the first, the repository policy, attestations and trusted runners
for the second. The courses Baton ships assume no particular architecture and serve the simpler end.
A factory whose guarantees rest on its own architecture writes the courses that rely on it and
keeps them with that factory: immediately.run's are Fieldhouse's (the owner, 2026-10-29).

## Who it is for

Teams that run many coding agents — or agents and people — against the same repositories, and
the operator who has to know at a glance what the factory is doing and what it needs.

## What it is not

- **Not the record of the work.** Git holds every ticket fact and its evidence; Baton holds
  the live leases and the limits agents cannot change, and defers to git about tickets.
- **Not a workflow engine.** Baton runs no repository code; workers decide how to do a leg.
  For durable execution of your own code, use something like Temporal
  ([compared](../compared-to-temporal.mdx)).
- **Not tied to GitHub.** Any git forge, or a plain git server, will do.
