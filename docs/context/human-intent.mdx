<Lede>Baton coordinates people, agents and deterministic code on the same tickets. Its safeguards
exist because much of that work runs with no human watching. A person, though, holds the
credentials — inference, the forge, the cloud — to do anything outside Baton that Baton could
forbid inside it. So Baton's aim is not to straitjacket people: it is to make mixed human, agentic
and deterministic workflows **predictable, observable and safe**. This page says how Baton tells
the two apart and what changes when a human drives (the owner, 2026-11-25 and 2026-11-26).</Lede>

The rules here are the owner's decisions and stand now. Most of the mechanisms that carry them —
interactive and autonomous delegated keys, session claims — are
[proposed, not built](#what-is-built-and-what-is-proposed); until they are, a rule that names
them is the design those mechanisms follow.

## 1. Who drives is a property of the acting credential

Baton cannot observe who is typing: an agent session making calls looks the same whether a person
steers it or it runs in a loop. So "human-driven" is defined by **the credential that acts**, as a
capability is: a statement Baton records and matches and never verifies ([values](values.mdx)
#12).

| A credential that carries… | Is | Its acts are attributed to |
|---|---|---|
| **a person's identity** | **human-driven** | that person |
| **a runner and a purpose** (generally one leg run) | **autonomous** | that runner's run |

- **Human-driven credentials:** a dashboard session (someone signed in); an owner key used from a
  person's own terminal; an **interactive** delegated (MCP) key; a runner key granted to a person,
  the runner declaring `human-operator`.
- **Autonomous credentials:** a runner key without `human-operator`; an **autonomous** delegated
  key.
- **An interactive key bakes in its holder's identity** and is minted only for oneself: no one
  mints a key that acts as someone else. **An autonomous key carries its runner and its purpose**
  — a leg run, an intervention — is scoped to what that purpose needs, and dies with it.
- **An agent leg a person joins stays agent-driven.** Its actor is the runner that claimed it; the
  person who opens its session link (R-BTN-195) holds no credential in Baton and acts in the
  harness, not in Baton. Joining a session is not taking it over.

## 2. How strongly intent is proven is a separate axis

Who drives decides how Baton **behaves**. How much proof an act needs that a human made it — a
dashboard sign-in, a `human_only` key, a hardware key — is the arena's **policy**, per action
([BATON_SPEC §12.8](../../spec/BATON_SPEC/12-security.md#sec-12-8)). The two never stand in for
each other: no rule reads a proof level as the driver, and no rule reads the driver as a proof.
Under `cooperative` the identity a credential carries is enough; a stricter policy may ask a
human-driven act for a stronger proof, and the act is refused, not reclassified, without it.

## 3. What a human-driven act may relax, and what it never does

**It may relax the safeguards that exist for autonomous work**, because the same act done outside
Baton would not meet them either:

- creating a ticket and claiming it at once — for an autonomous credential, remote code execution;
  for a person, what they could do with their own forge credential;
- loosening what a delegated key may otherwise only tighten (R-BTN-186);
- filing a ticket that is not a proposal (`tickets.created: proposed` is for autonomous filers);
- approving content it wrote: work a human-driven session wrote and committed is approved by that
  act, unless the policy asks for a stronger proof (section 2).

**It never relaxes two things:**

- **The lock.** One holder per ticket, the lease and the fence protect everyone else working
  alongside — other people, other agents — not the work; and a person outside Baton cannot break
  them either, since they simply do not hold the lease. A human-driven claim is refused where any
  claim is, by the gates that protect the lock.
- **The record.** Every act is recorded with who drove it and the proof it carried. Allowed never
  means invisible: an act a person made directly is as findable afterwards as one a runner made.

## 4. Channels are not drivers

MCP, the CLI, the HTTP API and the dashboard are channels. The same channel serves both kinds of
credential, with different behaviour:

- With an **autonomous** credential, an act beyond its scope is a **request** a person confirms —
  on Baton's own page, or through a delivery runner's channel (ntfy, Slack) that links there.
- With a **human-driven** credential, the person **acts directly**: no confirmation step stands
  between them and an act their credential may take.

## 5. The caveat, and why it does not change the rule

In a human-driven agent session, the agent issues the calls, and it reads text anyone who can push
wrote. An injected instruction can act through the person's session. That is no different from
what the same agent can already do with the person's forge and inference credentials outside
Baton: protecting a person's own session is the harness's work (its permission prompts), not
Baton's. Baton's part is the record, so the act is found afterwards, and a warning where a person
connects an agent to it: a fresh session that holds nothing else is strongly advised.

## 6. People

A person is an entity of its own: an id, the keys they authenticate with, and the identities
linked to them (forge accounts, emails, tracker accounts, contact identifiers). **Roles belong to
people**, per arena — owner, and contributor for everyone else who starts courses, claims,
answers and is notified — and **keys prove who is acting**. Several people may each declare a
forge identity no agent uses, and a person's session claims have no ceiling beyond the lock (the
owner, 2026-11-26; [people, roles and identities](../../spec/proposals/people-and-dashboard.md#5-people-roles-and-identities)).
A person's secrets are sealed on their own machine and never readable by Baton
([personal secrets](../../spec/proposals/personal-secrets.md)).

## What is built and what is proposed

- **Built:** runner keys and their grants; owner and administrator keys; dashboard sessions; the
  `human-operator` capability; `human_only` keys and the policy's human-only lists.
- **Proposed:** delegated MCP keys at all, and their interactive and autonomous kinds
  ([people and dashboard](../../spec/proposals/people-and-dashboard.md)); session claims held by an
  interactive key, and creating then claiming a ticket in one session
  ([ticket ownership](../../spec/proposals/ticket-ownership.md)); interventions split by driver
  ([intervention sessions](../../spec/proposals/intervention-sessions.md)); people as entities,
  with roles and linked identities ([people and dashboard §5](../../spec/proposals/people-and-dashboard.md#5-people-roles-and-identities));
  sealed personal secrets and notification preferences ([personal secrets](../../spec/proposals/personal-secrets.md)).
- **To amend when built:** R-BTN-186 (tighten-only binds autonomous delegated keys, not
  interactive ones), R-BTN-189's guarantees that read "a human" (they mean a human-driven act at
  the proof the policy names), and the [vocabulary](vocabulary.mdx)'s *Delegated key*,
  *Human-only key* and *Capability* entries, so each names one axis.
