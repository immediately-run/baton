<Lede>Baton coordinates people, agents and deterministic code on the same tickets. Its safeguards
exist because much of that work runs with no human watching. A person, though, holds the
credentials — inference, the forge, the cloud — to do anything outside Baton that Baton could
forbid inside it. So Baton's aim is not to straitjacket people: it is to make mixed human, agentic
and deterministic workflows **predictable, observable and safe**. This page says how Baton tells
the two apart and what changes when a person drives (the owner, 2026-11-25 and 2026-11-26).</Lede>

The rules here are the owner's decisions and stand now. Most of the mechanisms that carry them —
interactive MCP grants, session claims, people — are
[proposed, not built](#what-is-built-and-what-is-proposed); until they are, a rule that names
them is the design those mechanisms follow.

## 1. The key decides who drives, never the channel

Baton cannot observe who is typing: an agent making calls looks the same whether a person steers
it or it runs in a loop. So who drives is decided by **the credential that acts**, a statement
Baton records and matches and never verifies ([values](values.mdx) #12).

| A credential that carries… | Is | Its acts are attributed to |
|---|---|---|
| **a person** | **human-driven** | that person |
| **a runner and a purpose** (generally one leg run) | **agent-driven** | that runner's run |

The channel says nothing about it:

| Channel | Who drives |
|---|---|
| The dashboard | always a person: someone signed in. For a human-only list, a session counts as `human` only as strongly as its sign-in proves a person (the owner, 2026-11-26; R-BTN-185 as [#451](https://github.com/immediately-run/baton/pull/451) amends it — today every session counts): under `human_key: any`, only one Cloudflare Access opened or a `human_only` key's code opened, never an ordinary key's link or code, nor a forge sign-in, since agents use forge credentials too; under `hardware`, every session, each act with its touch (§2). |
| The CLI | the key's: a person's own SSH key is human-driven; a runner's key, in a sandbox or anywhere, is agent-driven. |
| MCP | the credential's: a person's **interactive grant** is human-driven; a key minted for an agent's purpose is agent-driven. |

- **A person's credentials are their own.** A person registers their own keys and signs in for
  themselves; no one gets a credential that acts as someone else. Claude Code driven by a person
  uses that person's credential, and its acts are the person's.
- **An interactive MCP grant is short-lived.** It is an OAuth grant a person approves for their
  own agent ([BATON_MCP_OAUTH_SPEC](../../spec/BATON_MCP_OAUTH_SPEC.md), amended): a short-lived
  access token, its refresh capped and ended by idleness, so a grant left in an agent's
  configuration does not outlive the work it was made for. No long-lived bearer key is
  human-driven. A person who owns no key signs in with the forge to get a session.
- **A person's own CLI holds a claim without becoming a runner.** `baton session claim` keeps a
  claim's lease and installs the `pre-push` fence for the person's own interactive session, and
  runs no queued legs ([ticket ownership](../../spec/proposals/ticket-ownership.md#session-claims)).
- **A runner is always agent-driven.** A runner key carries a runner. `human-operator` is a
  runner's own statement, matched to route legs that want a person at the runner's terminal; it
  never makes the runner human-driven. But today a runner's legs can use its host's `ssh-agent`
  (R-BTN-107): on a machine that also holds a person's keys, a leg can sign with the person's key,
  and by this rule its acts are then human-driven — creating and claiming at once, and whatever
  governance acts that key admits. That is an accepted risk; keeping legs from a person's keys is in
  the [hardening proposal](../../spec/proposals/hardening.md).
- **An agent-driven key carries a purpose**, and its runner where it has one — a leg run, an
  intervention, an automation an owner minted it for — is scoped to what that purpose needs, and
  ends with it.
- **An agent leg a person joins stays agent-driven.** Its actor is the runner that claimed it; the
  person who opens its session link (R-BTN-195) acts in the harness, not in Baton. Joining a
  session is not taking it over. (An **autonomous leg** is another thing: a leg that never asks for
  a person at all, R-BTN-195.)
- **A forge identity never says who drives.** A forge cannot tell a person's push from their
  agent's. A forge account linked to a person says *which* person — attribution — never that a
  person, not an agent, acted. The policy's `human_identity` (R-BTN-188) stays what the spec makes
  it: a promise of custody, which Baton cannot check.

## 2. How strongly a person must prove it is a separate axis

Who drives decides how Baton **behaves**. How much proof an act needs is the arena's **policy**,
per action ([BATON_SPEC §12.8](../../spec/BATON_SPEC/12-security.md#sec-12-8)); its approver lists
already speak in proofs: `[owner]` admits any owner key, `[human]` a `human_only` key kept out of
every agent's reach, and the policy may require that key to be a hardware key. (A grant is approved
from a session and never exceeds it: an act a session takes only once its key confirms it
([R-BTN-155](../../spec/BATON_SPEC/11-people.md#sec-11-4)) waits for that confirmation from a grant too, and [R-BTN-186](../../spec/BATON_SPEC/12-security.md#sec-12-8)'s key-only acts — minting a
credential among them — are never a grant's.)

| Credential | The proof it carries |
|---|---|
| a dashboard session | the sign-in that opened it; under `hardware`, a touch for each act |
| a request the CLI signs with an SSH key | that key; a hardware key, a touch for each signature |
| an MCP bearer or OAuth token | the token alone: whose credential it is, never that the person was present |

The two axes never stand in for each other: no rule reads a proof as the driver or the driver as a
proof. Under `cooperative`, a person's interactive grant is admitted wherever their key's role is
— governance acts on an `[owner]` list included. Under `protected`, a list that requires a
`human_only` or hardware key refuses a token for its proof, not for its channel, and the act is
refused, never reclassified.

## 3. What a human-driven act may relax, and what it never does

**It may relax the safeguards that exist for agent-driven work**, because the same act done
outside Baton would not meet them either:

- creating a ticket and claiming it at once — for an agent-driven credential, remote code
  execution; for a person, what they could do with their own forge credential, or, on a roster
  of `org-members`, what the arena's owners chose to trust every member of the organisation with;
- what a delegated key may otherwise only tighten (R-BTN-186), up to what the person's own role
  and proof admit;
- filing a ticket that is not a proposal (`tickets.created: proposed` is for agent-driven filers);
- approving content it wrote: work a human-driven session wrote and committed is approved by that
  act at the proof it carries, unless the policy asks for a stronger one (§2).

**It never relaxes two things:**

- **The lock.** One holder per ticket, the lease and the fence protect everyone else working
  alongside — other people, other agents — not the work; and a person outside Baton cannot break
  them either, since they simply do not hold the lease. A human-driven claim is refused where any
  claim is, by the gates that protect the lock.
- **The record.** Every act is recorded with who drove it and the proof it carried. Allowed never
  means invisible: an act a person made directly is as findable afterwards as one a runner made.

## 4. An agent-driven act beyond its scope

Today Baton refuses it (`forbidden`). Where an intervention runs agent-driven
([proposed](../../spec/proposals/intervention-sessions.md)), such an act becomes a **request** an
approver confirms on Baton's own page, reached from the page or from a delivery runner's channel
(ntfy, Slack) that links there. A human-driven credential files no request: the person acts
directly, within what their role and proof admit.

## 5. The accepted risk

In a human-driven agent session the agent issues the calls, and it reads text anyone who can push
wrote; an injected instruction acts through the person's credential. Outside Baton that agent
already holds the person's forge and inference credentials. **Inside Baton it gains reach those do
not give**: holds, adopts, expedites, and whatever governance acts the person's role admits at the
proof the credential carries. Some of those **outlive the session**: an owner key added, an agent
key minted, a notifier key listed. And a person's key used by an agent nobody watches — a
scheduled supervising agent holding the owner's SSH key — is human-driven all the same, because
the key decides. Baton accepts this rather than ranking channels, and limits it three ways:
interactive grants are short-lived (§1); a policy that wants a governance act made only with a
human-only or hardware key says so, and a token then cannot make it (§2); and every act is
recorded as the person's. An authority-changing act made with a grant also waits, as a
session's does, for its key's confirmation (R-BTN-155), and no grant mints a credential (§2). A
proof level that admits key-signed requests but not tokens, for
governance acts, is in the [hardening proposal](../../spec/proposals/hardening.md). Protecting a person's own session is the
harness's work (its permission prompts). Where a person connects an agent to Baton, Baton warns
that a fresh session holding nothing else is strongly advised.

## 6. People

A person is for **accountability and notification**: the credentials that are theirs, so every act
is attributed; the identities linked to them; and how to reach them. A person confers **no
authority**: roles stay on keys as the spec has them (owner keys, `human_only` keys, runner grants),
and the policy's lists name roles, never people or groups. Access beyond Baton's own lock and
policy is the forge's: an arena's **roster** — who may start a course or hold a session claim —
is read from the forge by the arena's sync (the accounts that can push to its config repository,
or the members of the forge organisation that owns it, as the arena chooses), never a list Baton
keeps. A person signs in with the forge, which links and verifies their forge account in one step ([people](../../spec/proposals/people-and-dashboard.md#5-people-identities-and-the-roster)).
A person's secrets are sealed on their own machine, never readable by Baton
([personal secrets](../../spec/proposals/personal-secrets.md)). Baton keeps no groups: notifying a
team is a pull from Needs you, or one push to a channel the team already shares.

## What is built and what is proposed

- **Built:** runner keys and their grants; owner and administrator keys; dashboard sessions for
  administrators (owners' and members' are specified, not built: R-BTN-155, R-BTN-204); the `human-operator` capability; `human_only` keys and the policy's human-only lists;
  `human_identity`.
- **Proposed:** MCP ([people and dashboard](../../spec/proposals/people-and-dashboard.md)), with interactive grants
  and agent-driven keys; people, their linked identities and the roster
  ([§5](../../spec/proposals/people-and-dashboard.md#5-people-identities-and-the-roster)); session
  claims, and creating then claiming a ticket at once
  ([ticket ownership](../../spec/proposals/ticket-ownership.md)); interventions split by driver
  ([intervention sessions](../../spec/proposals/intervention-sessions.md)); sealed personal secrets
  and notification ([personal secrets](../../spec/proposals/personal-secrets.md)).
- **To amend when built:** BATON_MCP_OAUTH_SPEC §5, §7 and §8 and G-BTN-157 (an interactive grant
  is a person's own, short-lived, acting with their role within the consent's ticks, and is not a
  delegated key; a grant scoped to an intervention stays agent-driven); R-BTN-181 (the writer
  `person`, for a person's grant and session claims); R-BTN-186 — its limits (tighten only, never as `human`, never resolving
  a condition, never writing an attestation or answering a person leg) bind agent-driven delegated
  keys, while an interactive grant acts with its person's role at its proof; R-BTN-188 ("a
  delegated key matches no entry" binds agent-driven keys; `tickets.created` speaks of agent-driven
  filers); R-BTN-130 (the two kinds of MCP credential); R-BTN-163 (resolving a condition); R-BTN-185
  (the owner's agent: a person's key used by their agent is human-driven at owner-key proof, and
  still never satisfies `[human]`; and which sessions act as `human` under `human_key: any`, unless
  [#451](https://github.com/immediately-run/baton/pull/451) lands it first), with R-BTN-188's
  `human` approver entry, which today admits any dashboard session; R-BTN-155 (an interactive
  grant's authority-changing acts wait for its key's confirmation, as its session's do); R-BTN-189's guarantees that read "a human" (they mean the proof
  they name); the Decisions entry on authority by credential; and the
  [vocabulary](vocabulary.mdx)'s *Delegated key*, *Human-only key* and *Capability* entries, so each
  names one axis.
