**Brief:** the owner, 2026-10-22, after the B-86 leg: "im more concerned with how we can detect
this in the future." The runner held a claim in `implement` for five hours past the agent's last
output with nothing to show the operator — every signal it logs was green.
**Spec:** §8.3 R-BTN-72 (the runner: the leg child and its waiting), §7's `baton.stalled`
(R-BTN-163, R-BTN-167's default timer — the server-side answer, still §7's proposal) and
R-BTN-90's deadlines; B-23 (the runner's leg-output archive) is the observability half. The
baton-stalled/… ticket:
**Depends on:** nothing — the runner-side logging stands alone; the condition lands with §7.

### What to build

1. **The leg child's output is piped, not ignored.** The leg child spawns with
   `stdio: ['pipe', 'ignore', 'ignore']` — the runner never sees the agent's stream. B-23's
   archive (masked per R-BTN-144) is the full answer; the detection half lands first: the
   runner timestamps every chunk it drains, so "last activity" is a fact.
2. **A liveness line each beat while a leg runs**: `baton: <ticket>'s <leg> running N min, last
   output M min ago` — the operator's `tail -f` shows the wedge forming instead of silence.
3. **A stalled warning**: a leg running past a threshold (default 60 min, the course's to
   raise) with no output and no checkout change for 10+ minutes logs the B-86-shaped warning
   naming the claim and the agent's pid — the signature `scripts/start-runner.sh` already
   watches for from outside; the runner knows it from inside, without pgrep.
4. **When §7 lands**, the warning becomes core's `baton.stalled` condition (R-BTN-167's timer
   fires, Needs you carries it) and R-BTN-90's deadline takes the claim — the runner-side line
   stays as the local view.

### Do not

- Kill the agent from the runner: a deadline is Baton's word (R-BTN-90: "Baton cannot stop a
  runner" — and a runner guessing about its agent is the same trap one level down). The
  warning tells the human; §7's machinery decides.

### Tests

- Unit: a leg child that prints, sleeps past the thresholds and prints again — the liveness
  lines and the one warning appear in order, and no warning while output flows.
- The B-86 shape replayed: a child that exits never — the warning fires once per leg.

### Exit criteria

- [ ] a wedged-post-completion agent is visible in the runner's log within minutes, not hours
- [ ] no warning while a leg is producing output, whatever its duration
