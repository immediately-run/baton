### Requires

A checkout and a terminal for most of it. Two exit criteria need forge access:
- a public GitHub repository, to exercise fork PRs and permission reads with a real token;
- a GitLab project, to read real `id_tokens` claims.

### Why

The owner, 2026-10-18, asked that the single-tenant findings of the security review be
addressed, and settled five choices: a multi-repository key opts in, exit-status verdicts,
MCP add-only holds, a loopback local host with `--bind`, and Developer as GitLab's "can
push".

### What to build

Split into reviewed PRs along four parts. Each is independent.

1. **People without push** (R-BTN-139 to R-BTN-142).
   - *Done by B-119:* GitHub's author trust — every author past the `author_association`
     pre-filter is read from `collaborators/{login}/permission` (`admin` or `write`), once per
     run. Editors, forks, `trusted_identities` and the other forges remain here.
   - The fact adapters' `trusted` flag and fork detection, by ids; `trusted_identities`; the
     default station functions read trusted facts only.
   - `code_repos`.
   - The branch-pattern grammar, and branch-holder scoping.
   - Literal bars and `renamed_from` collisions, with `baton owner bars follow`.
2. **Runners** (R-BTN-143 to R-BTN-145). Server side: `default_branch`, `rewrite_held` and
   `tainted_branches` in the claim response; the `off-branch` end, the `baton.off-branch`
   condition it raises (R-BTN-163) and its sync want. In `src/runner/`:
   - the ancestry check and the verified-sha cache, fetching the registered branch, not
     `main` (`home.ts`, `main.ts`);
   - `close` and `handoff` in a child;
   - a scrubbed leg environment, with `credential:` scoping;
   - the masking set and patterns; pointer `commit` and `sha256`; `baton leg log`
     verification;
   - the `IR_BATON_LEG` tag sweep, with a systemd scope where available;
   - the replay allowlist and `BATON_RESUME`.
3. **Keys, posts and claims** (R-BTN-146 to R-BTN-152).
   - Span, the grant's `sync` flag (its only flag), `GET …/grants` and `--confirm-span`.
   - `head_unfounded`, held posts that only tighten, the `baton.<sub>` conditions those rules
     raise (`baton.span-changed`, `baton.head-replaced`, `baton.renumbered` and the others they
     name), renumbering, and dropped claims.
   - Open-world `declared` gates, `misdeclared`, and tainted branches.
   - The per-repository caps, observation takeover, and readings and bills bounds.
   - `rank` output validation.
4. **Surfaces** (R-BTN-153 to R-BTN-158).
   - Check heads as core fields, claim-bound shas, confirmed ends, `exec` verdicts.
   - MCP add-only holds and `ticket.unhold`.
   - The sign-in code flow, logout and revoke.
   - CI-token `event_name`, `workflow_ref`, `ref_protected` and `pipeline_source`; the
     GitLab protected-variable check in `repo init`; notification binding.
   - Token limits, role expansion from the snapshot, and links.
   - The App manifest checks; backticks and the export fence.
   - The local host's `--bind` and file modes.
   - Import re-validation and the pre-seal refusal.
   - The signature profile, the headers, `trigger_workflows`, foreign claims after cutover,
     and the fence per incarnation.

5. **The migration.** One new numbered migration with §13's columns and tables for these rules.
   - Columns: the columns listed there, and `worker_grants.accepted_at`, backfilled to each
     grant's creation time so existing grants stay active.
   - Tables: `tainted_branches`, `open_world_bars`, `login_codes`, `write_budgets`,
     `observation_lapses` and `action_confirmations`.
   - Migrations are append-only, so `login_links` stays, unwritten.
   - The release notes name the cost: keys already active on two or more repositories lose
     machine sync until owners set each grant's `sync` flag. `baton owner grants list` names
     them beforehand.
6. **Rounds 2 and 3's additions** (each with its scenario):
   - pending grants, with `runner.grant.accept` and `runner.grant.decline` and their routes
     and commands;
   - the board post's `source: {kind, cli}`;
   - observation renewal, the 30-minute age and the sync key's or CI's void;
   - the observation response's `trusted_identities`, effective `code_repos` and
     `tainted_branches`;
   - confirmation codes for the dashboard's authority-changing actions, with `baton admin
     confirm` (and its `dry_run`);
   - the sign-in eviction order;
   - bearer refusal by peer on an `http` listener, `--trusted-proxy`, and the Cloudflare
     host's refusal on plain `http`;
   - the tainted-branch `pre-push` hook;
   - round 4's (the hardening rules' fourth look): the settlement outcome table; `check_head_ref` in the claim
     response and a sync want on a moved head; `pending_grants` in the heartbeat and the
     runner's hint; API-key and migrated grants active at once; decline returning an active
     grant to pending, logged; a hostname binding at first acceptance; the confirmation set
     without Stop or setting a hold; clients refusing to send bearers over non-loopback `http`
     and the server refusing every one that arrives (which it revokes is item 7's).
7. **The adversarial review's fixes** (2026-10-20), each with its scenario case:
   - **Author and posted-state gates at settlement** (R-BTN-104/R-BTN-148, adv-1 M7), in the
     settlement path in `src/core/claims.ts`. The author gates are the `requires` and tier gates,
     judged over `declared` at the claim and again over the posted ticket; the outcome table
     applies only when one of them fails. A claim settling at `start` whose author gates pass but
     whose posted-state gate fails for the claimed leg (not `ready`, a block, an open decision, a
     posted bar excluding the runner's tags) ends `released:stale-board`: no failed attempt, the
     branch tainted when the settling post is at the claim's sha.
   - **Consents in their own table** (R-BTN-140, adv-4 M2).
     - `code_repo_consents` `(home_id, repo_id)` with `consented_by` and `consented_at`, judged by
       id at every use.
     - It is written only by `POST /v1/owner/{repository}/code-repo-consents {repository,
       confirm: true}` or `{repository, withdraw: true}`, signed by the consenting repository's
       owner (a registry action and a `baton owner` command), or by an administrator's
       `code_repo_consents_add` on `POST /v1/admin/repositories`.
     - The owners' `code_repos` write takes `path` only. Baton resolves `repo_id` at addition and
       keeps `former_paths` itself; a `repo_id` or `consented_at` in the write is not kept, and a
       re-written list keeps the ids and consents of the paths it keeps.
     - An entry naming a live registered repository has no reach until that repository
       consents, and raises `baton.code-repo-unconsented`.
   - **The longest id wins a branch** (R-BTN-141, adv-1 M8). One matcher, used by the fact
     adapter, the standard station functions (`src/defaults/course-pr.ts`,
     `src/defaults/course-markers.ts`), the runner's local branch check and `branchHolder` in
     `src/core/claims.ts`: a branch maps to a ticket only when no longer id of the same repository
     also matches it. Until a child's first snapshot, its branch maps to its parent.
   - **No roll-back** (R-BTN-143, adv-3 F4). The claim response names `base_sha`
     (`board_snapshots.parent_sha`; null for a repository's first snapshot and for a held post
     accepted per R-BTN-11). The runner (`src/runner/home.ts`) checks that it is an ancestor of
     `snapshot_sha` too, and a failure is handled as an off-branch sha. The held machine-key
     post that may name such a sha is B-77's.
   - **Accepting a grant** (R-BTN-146, §12.1, §14; adv-4 m2, m4).
     - `POST /v1/runner/grants/{repository}/accept` takes `{confirm_span?}`; an acceptance that
       raises the span without it is `conflict`, listing the repositories that would lose sync.
     - A machine key whose every grant is pending and never accepted holds no role, so a grant
       naming any public key reserves nothing; its acceptance is checked as a registration.
     - The one-role `conflict` (`src/core/admin.ts`) never names the role the key holds.
   - **The declaration budget** (§2.3, R-BTN-149, adv-1 m8). A heartbeat's declaration and
     report writes count per credential per UTC day. Past 50,000, the heartbeat still renews the
     lease, but its declaration changes and reports are dropped and it is answered
     `declarations: 'quota'` until 00:00 UTC. The count lives in this item's migration.
   - **Revoking a bearer over `http`** (R-BTN-158, adv-4 M3). Only a bearer whose secret fully
     verifies is revoked (a cookie's session ended) and raises `baton.bearer-over-http`. One that
     does not verify is refused `unauthenticated` and counted in §2.3's failure buckets: an id
     or fingerprint alone, which is public, revokes nothing.
   - **Access is never read** (R-BTN-16, adv-4 m5). Remove `verifyOwner` and every
     `Cf-Access-Jwt-Assertion` read from `src/core/auth.ts` and `src/core/front.ts`, the
     `X-Baton-Owner` principal from `src/core/baton.ts`, and the `ACCESS_TEAM_DOMAIN`,
     `ACCESS_AUD`, `OWNER_EMAILS` and `TEST_ACCESS_JWKS` bindings (`src/core/ports.ts`,
     `src/local/main.ts`, `src/local/testCourse.ts`, the test bindings). A dashboard request is
     authorized by Baton's own session alone. R3-1208 narrows the Access policy itself. The
     release notes say that a deployment signing in through Access alone needs an administrator
     key first.

### Architecture

- **Forge reads stay in the CLI**, never core (§1, R-BTN-50). Core learns only the `trusted` result
  through the posted facts, and the new owner settings.
- **Fail closed** wherever a forge read is refused: no trusted author, no counted PR, no
  verdict.
- **No signatures and no per-runner identities** (R-BTN-46).

### Tests

S-BTN-115 to S-BTN-125 in `test/integration/`, on both hosts, with unit tests for each
adapter's trust and fork mapping from real payload shapes. Item 7's cases, on both hosts:
- S-BTN-116: A's owner writing `code_repos` with a `repo_id` and `consented_at` for B keeps only
  the path, with no reach until B's owner consents; a re-written list keeps its ids and consents.
- S-BTN-121: an open-world claim settling at its sha with an open decision ends `stale-board`,
  not failed, with its branch tainted. This ticket writes only that case; the lag, held-row and
  `stream` cases are B-77's.
- S-BTN-122: heartbeats flipping declarations past the budget keep their leases and drop the
  declarations (`declarations: 'quota'`).
- S-BTN-123: a post naming an older default-branch sha gives `released:off-branch`, because
  `base_sha` is not its ancestor.
- S-BTN-124: an unverifying bearer naming an administrator's `bak_<id>` over `http` is refused
  and nothing is revoked.
- S-BTN-32: a pending grant on a key later added as an owner gives `conflict` without naming the
  role. Its `admin.key.revoke` case is B-76's.
- Unit: `claude/T-12-1-impl` maps to `T-12-1`, never `T-12`, in every caller of the matcher, and
  to `T-12` while `T-12-1` is unposted; `accept` without `confirm_span` listing the repositories;
  no `Cf-Access-Jwt-Assertion` header changes any principal.

### Exit criteria

- `npm test` and `npm run typecheck` green, with S-BTN-115 to S-BTN-125 and G-BTN-135 to
  G-BTN-145 passing, item 7's cases among them; G-BTN-57's pending-grant clause and G-BTN-59's
  open-world clause hold.
- No code under `src/` reads Cloudflare Access's cookie or JWT.
- On a scratch public GitHub repository, an outsider's fork PR and approve comment move
  nothing.
- A real GitLab `id_tokens` JWT from a merge-request pipeline is refused.
