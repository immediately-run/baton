**Spec:** R-BTN-19 (*fence before re-issue*), §9.5 (*Reaping*), R-BTN-9 (the lease timeout's
clamp), R-BTN-26 (the sidecar's own fence), R-BTN-178 (seats held until `fence-cleared`), §12.2
(destructive actions), G-BTN-14, S-BTN-4, S-BTN-21, S-BTN-50. Source:
`docs/review/security-review-2026-11-02.mdx`, finding S3, checked against the text in PR #165.

### Why

A claim reaped while its runner was alive — a force-reap, an unacknowledged-release reap, a
`leg-timeout` reap — is fence-pending until that incarnation reports `fenced` or until
`fence_deadline`, the reap plus the runner's declared `lease_timeout_ms`, which R-BTN-9 clamps to
at most 86,400 s. `baton.fence-pending` at 30 minutes is a notice, not a control; rule 3 refuses
every claim meanwhile; adopt and force-reap act only on active claims; the registry has no
fence action. So an administrator who force-reaps a hung laptop runner, and knows the machine is
off, waits up to a day for the ticket, and its capacity seats stay held as long (R-BTN-178). A
hostile runner turns its own reap into a day's hold on the ticket.

### Decide (the owner)

- **(a) recommended — an administrator's fence-clear.** A new registry action,
  `claim.fence-clear` (`POST /v1/ui/claims/{id}/fence-clear` and `baton admin claim fence-clear
  <claim>`), destructive under §12.2, administrator only, never delegated: it clears the fence at
  once, frees the seats, and logs `fence-cleared {admin: <fingerprint>}`. The dialog shows the
  runner's last-seen time and says that a process the old incarnation still runs can push until
  its own lease check fails (`last_ok + lease_timeout − 4 min`, R-BTN-26), and that R-BTN-25's
  re-read is then the only protection. The deadline stays as it is.
- (b) bound the deadline: `fence_deadline` = reap + min(`lease_timeout`, 2 h). Needs no new
  action, but breaks the deadline's stated reason — a runner declaring a long lease (a sleeping
  laptop) may still hold processes whose own checks have not yet computed the lease lost — so
  it trades the fence's guarantee for availability for every runner, not only the one an
  administrator vouches for.
- (c) both.

### What to build (for (a))

1. **A spec PR first** under the babysit skill's *Spec PRs*: R-BTN-19 names the clear; §9.5
   lists it with force-reap; §12.2 and the registry (R-BTN-129) gain the action; G-BTN-14 reads
   "until `fence-cleared`, its `fence_deadline` or an administrator's clear, which nothing the
   runner does extends"; S-BTN-4 gains the clear (claimable at once, seats freed, the event logged)
   and an owner's and a delegated key's attempt (`forbidden`).
2. The core action, the route, the CLI verb and the dashboard control, generated from the
   registry entry; a seal (S-BTN-50) that waits on a fence-pending claim proceeds after a clear.
