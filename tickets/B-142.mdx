**Brief:** the owner, 2026-10-30, in the conversation recorded in `spec/BATON_SPEC_HISTORY.md`:
Baton must not hard-code `cooperative` and `protected`; every rule they bundled is a knob of a
per-repository policy, presets are conveniences, and the policy is the interface through which
Baton enforces Fieldhouse's guarantees.
**Spec:** `spec/BATON_SPEC.md` §12.8 R-BTN-188 (the repository policy), R-BTN-189 (guarantees,
floors and signed reads), R-BTN-180 (trust levels and protection, now read from the policy),
R-BTN-185 (human gates as `human` approvers), R-BTN-186 (delegated keys), R-BTN-66 (what lives in
Baton); §13 `repository_policies`, `policy_floors`; §14 the four policy routes; §15 S-BTN-189,
S-BTN-190, G-BTN-163, G-BTN-164.
**Depends on:** nothing to start. The knobs each bind the ticket that builds their mechanism:
B-140 (the merge runner reads `merge`, `paths`, `authorship`), B-137 (`inputs.board`), B-64
(`outbox`), B-124 and B-136 (approvers and human-only keys). Whichever of those lands first reads
its knob from this ticket's store if it exists, and from a fixed default otherwise.

### Requires

None: a checkout and a terminal.

### Why

Today the spec decides fifteen rules by one switch, `landing`, and a few more by `authorship`,
`machine_sync` and `outbox_policy`. A repository cannot let a runner commit to main and still
require signed readings, and Fieldhouse cannot state, or have Baton keep, exactly the guarantees
it relies on (R-FH-3 names a mode).

### What to build

1. **Schema.** A new numbered migration adds `repository_policies` `(repository_id, version)`
   (`policy_json`, `hash`, `previous_hash`, `changed_by`, `approver`, `preset`, `at`; no update or
   delete) and `policy_floors` `(repository_id, name)` (`guarantees` JSON, `remove_by`,
   `added_by`, `added_at`). Every existing repository gets version 1, the `cooperative` preset,
   with its current `machine_sync` folded into `inputs.board`. The retired columns stay readable
   until no code reads them (append-only migrations).
2. **Core module `src/core/policy.ts`.** The knob table as data: each knob's JSON schema;
   `diff(old, new)` listing the knobs a change touches; the presets as documents;
   `guarantees(policy)` computing R-BTN-189's table over the policy, with the protection report
   applied separately. No knob has an order: every change needs `approvers.policy`. A validator in `src/core/validate.ts` refuses an unknown knob, a value
   outside its schema, an `outbox.git` class `refs.default_branch` excludes under `filed`, and,
   for a type `merge` or a trusted leg acts on, an `attestations` writer other than `human`,
   `baton` and `service:` keys holding no owner, runner or courier role (checked again at every
   course snapshot).
3. **Changes.** `PUT /v1/owner/{repository}/policy` in one `store.transaction`: compare-and-set
   on `base_version`; the approver check (`approvers.policy` for every change, never a delegated
   key; R-BTN-186, R-BTN-188); every floor re-checked against the new version (`floor-violated`); refusing
   a `human` approver, `human_key: hardware` or `remove_by: human` with no owner's `human_only`
   key to meet it; on `approvers.trust` becoming `human` (owner key only), every owner-set trust
   level cleared to `untrusted`, roles dropped, domains and families null; an `admin` event with
   both versions and `changed`. `GET …/policy/log`, and the floors route.
4. **Readers.** Every rule that read `landing`, `authorship`, `protected_paths`, `machine_sync`
   or `outbox_policy` reads its knob through `policy.ts`. No code compares a preset name.
5. **The signed read and the claim response.** `GET /v1/repositories/{repository}/policy`, signed
   with the tenant's `SigningKey`; `policy_version` and `policy_hash` on every claim response.
6. **The CLI.** `baton owner policy show | diff | log | set <file> | apply <preset> | floor add |
   floor remove`; trusted runners' pins of the policy and floors hashes, raising
   `baton.policy-unpinned`. No runner-side `--require`: Baton itself refuses claims and advances on
   a repository whose floored guarantee is broken (rule `policy`, `policy-broken`) and releases
   running claims with reason `policy` (`released:policy`), the protection checker exempt.
7. **`baton repo protect`** derives `--print` from `refs.*` and `paths.control`. Trusted runners
   report `--check` in their heartbeat's `protection`, which alone feeds `no-runner-lands`; their
   `policy_pins` drive `baton.policy-unpinned`; `released:policy` joins the end reasons.
8. **Export.** R-BTN-88's sections gain `repository_policies` and `policy_floors`; an import
   verifies the `previous_hash` chain and never falls back to a preset.
9. **Readings per repository.** A requirement reads state computed from the readings its
   repository's `readings` entry admits, signed ones only for a gate under `signed: true`; the
   reading routes and `instance_readings` gain `writer`, `nonce` and `signature`.
10. **Couriers' trust.** `couriers` gains `trust_level` and `trust_domain`, set by the
   `approvers.trust` approver.
11. **After the second review** (R-BTN-180, R-BTN-185, R-BTN-188): the protection checker role
   (no merge credential, never a workflow) as the only source of `protection` reports; the
   proposal merge; delegated keys refused on every policy route, and the owner's 24-hour restore;
   `trust_set_by`; floors judged on policy conditions alone; approver lists (`owner`, `human`,
   `key:<fingerprint>`) authorised by `approvers.policy` as it stands, with the satisfiable-list
   and last-key refusals (S-BTN-192); `floors_hash`; the `*` readings
   entry; `BATON_HUMAN_KEYS` and `POST /v1/human-keys` with the last-key refusal; S-BTN-191,
   G-BTN-165.

### Do not

- Store or compare a preset name anywhere but the change log's `preset` column.
- Read any knob from git, a board post or a course file.
- Let a delegated key loosen, or let a floor be removed by anyone but its `remove_by` approver.
- Put a deployment duty Baton cannot observe (a VM reset, review legs without branch code, where
  keys live) in the policy or in a core guarantee: those are the deployment's.
