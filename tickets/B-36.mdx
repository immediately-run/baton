**Brief:** the owner, 2026-10-18 (this session): "a whole ticket should be assigned to a user so that they can carry out all the human-in-the-loop requests and interactive legs because they are working on the particular ticket"; "the default should be anybody with access to the given repository"; "it should be assignable to a person or a group"; and channel-specific courier configuration (personal couriers are since retired, R-BTN-164).
**Spec:** `spec/BATON_SPEC.md` §6.4 R-BTN-78 (posted decisions), R-BTN-116 (person legs); §4.6 R-BTN-86 (children); §4.4 (the ticket's posted fields); §7.2 R-BTN-161 (the handler ticket and `std:condition@1`); §7.9 R-BTN-169 (`ask`, `deliver`, `announce`); §7.3 R-BTN-163 (the debugger and the floor); §3.4 R-BTN-96 (the sync reads the forge). This ticket amends them; the amendment is its first deliverable.
**Depends on:** B-7 (person legs and `asks`), B-58 (bindings and handler tickets, whose `std:condition@1` input this ticket extends). The delivery half (`ask`, `deliver` and `announce` addressing the assignee) is wired by whichever of this ticket and B-66 lands second.

### Requires

None: a checkout and a terminal.

### Why

A course's person legs, posted decisions, `hitl` conditions and B-34's interactive legs are addressed today by a role label (`asks: owner`) and reach whoever the bound handler course delivers to. Nothing says which person is carrying a given ticket. In a product build (brief → spec → roadmap → build), the person who defines the product should get every question that ticket raises, and should be the one whose session runs its interactive legs.

### What to build

1. **`assignee` on a ticket** (§4.4's posted fields), set in git by the ticket source. An identity is typed, `<provider>:user:<name>` or `<provider>:group:<name>`, so a person and a team never collide:
   - `github:user:joe`: a forge user;
   - `github:group:wiki-editors`: a forge team, in the organization that owns the ticket's home repository, or `github:group:<org>/<team>` to name another;
   - `baton:group:product`: a group recorded in git (2 below).

   Unset is the default and changes nothing: anyone with access to the repository may answer, and handlers deliver by the question's `asks` label as before.
2. **Groups, two kinds, both first-class.**
   - **In git:** `.baton/baton.json` `groups: {"<name>": ["github:user:<login>", …]}`, versioned and reviewed like the repository's other settings. A group's members are users, never other groups in v1. An unknown group or a malformed member fails the sync, naming the ticket.
   - **Forge-native teams:** the sync resolves a `github:group:…` assignee's members through the forge, as it reads PRs (R-BTN-96), and posts them with the ticket, so Baton itself still calls no forge.
     - Reading a team's members needs a token with organization read (a GitHub App's `members: read`, or a fine-grained token). The workflow's default `GITHUB_TOKEN` cannot read it.
     - A team the sync cannot read does not fail the board. The assignee is posted unresolved and the ticket raises a `notify` core condition (`baton.assignee-unresolved`, the name the amendment fixes) with the reason, closed when a later sync resolves it.
     - Membership is read on every sync, so a change of team takes effect at the next one.
3. **The assignee rides in the handler's input.** `std:condition@1` (R-BTN-161) gains `assignee: {id, members?}`: the raising ticket's assignee and, for a group, its members as the latest snapshot posted them, which the claim response carries to the runner writing the handler ticket (and the outbox's couriers, B-64, for core conditions). `ask`, `deliver` and `announce` (R-BTN-169) address the assignee's members when it is set, else the `asks` label. Baton routes nothing (R-BTN-125): who is reached is the handler course's business, and the floor (R-BTN-163) lists every open question whoever is assigned.
4. **Interactive legs (B-34)** are a leg capability, `interactive-session:assignee`, which core resolves per ticket to the assignee's identity (a member's, for a group; anyone's when unassigned). B-34 builds the resolution and the grant check. This ticket supplies the assignee and the group membership it resolves against.
5. **Children** inherit their parent's assignee unless they set their own; a handler ticket (R-BTN-161) inherits its raising ticket's.
6. **Reassignment is a git edit.** It takes effect from the snapshot that posts it: later raises carry the new assignee, and a running leg keeps its claim. An open handler ticket keeps the assignee it was filed with; a repository that wants it re-delivered re-raises.
7. **Who answered.** Routing only, in v1: anyone with access may still answer, and the answer's `answered_by` is recorded. Enforcing that only the assignee answers is left for later, as a filter (R-BTN-46), not a boundary.

### Open, to settle in the amendment

- **Baton's own conditions** (`baton.retries`, `baton.contract`, `baton.stalled`) on an assigned ticket. Recommended: their handler's input carries the assignee too, and the floor lists them for the owners as it does today.

### Do not

- Register channels or routing settings in Baton (severity minimums, digests, `remind_after`): notices for people and personal couriers are retired (R-BTN-164; history Migrations), and a channel's details live in the delivery runner's configuration.
- Gate claims or answers on the assignee except through B-34's capability.

### Tests

- A ticket assigned to a person, and one to a group: a `hitl` raise's handler ticket carries `assignee` and, for the group, its members; an unassigned ticket's carries none.
- A forge team's members are resolved by the sync and posted with the ticket. A team the sync's token cannot read is posted unresolved and raises `baton.assignee-unresolved`, and the board still posts. An unknown `baton:` group fails the sync, naming the ticket. Reassignment changes the next raise's `assignee`.
- A child and a handler ticket inherit the assignee.
- An interactive leg on an assigned ticket refuses a runner of anyone else. A group's second interactive leg goes to the member who took the first.
- A scenario (next free S-BTN number) through the local host, with its G-BTN row.
