**Brief:** the owner, 2026-11-22, deciding the question this ticket asks: **bounded serving**. Keep every runner row and all stored transcript pointers and status reports; serve the latest transcript and report only for recently-seen runners (the `runners_live` set), with the latest-of fields `null` beyond it, so the Runners view's three reads key by that set and `rows_read` per poll is bounded by a constant. No retention or pruning in this ticket. The implement agent on 2026-11-22 recommended the same; build it and open the PR.

**What:** B-250 (PR #281) made the dashboard's Runners view read one statement per list, however many runners were ever registered. Three of those lists are whole-table walks keyed by nothing — `transcripts`, `pending_transcripts`, and `messages WHERE kind = 'status_report'` through 0063's partial `messages_reports` — because the only key a latest-per-runner read has is the runners ever registered, the very count B-250 stopped paying. Nothing prunes those tables: R-BTN-149's retention ([§2.3](../../spec/BATON_SPEC/02-deployments.md#sec-2-3), src/core/retention.ts) deletes a long-unseen runner's declarations, events and snapshots past 90 days, and the claims of purged tickets with their transcripts — a kept claim's transcript pointers stay forever, and no `DELETE FROM messages` exists at all; the only `pending_transcripts` delete is linking (src/core/claims.ts). The view's `rows_read` per poll therefore grows with those tables over the object's life, on the same road to Cloudflare's free-tier cap of 5,000,000 `rows_read`/day that production passed on 2026-11-14 and 2026-11-15 (B-250's Why). The B-240 harness state (300 old runners carrying no transcripts or reports) stays inside the 500-row target; months of history do not.

- **An owner decision first, before the build:** bound the tables (retention for old transcript pointers and status reports), or bound the serving (the latest transcript and report served only for recently-seen runners, so the three reads key by a bounded runner set). Retention makes an old runner's `transcript_url` and `last_report` null — a behaviour change with its own scenario and dashboard reading; bounded serving changes what old runners are served without deleting anything.
- **The retention option** follows B-238's shape: bounded batches in the alarm (at most `RETENTION_BATCH` rows a tick), oldest first, each prune through an index, a tick with nothing to delete reading a few rows. Consider whether B-259's export-before-delete applies here as it does to events: the owner may want old pointers and reports exported before deletion.
- **The bounded-serving option** keys the three reads by the recently-seen set (the `runners_live` partial index's set), nulling the latest-of fields beyond it; the served rows themselves stay, as R-BTN-3 and the dashboard's every-row rendering keep them.

**Target:** the three history lookups read at most a constant number of rows per live runner per poll, independent of the size of the retained tables. The roster still costs at least one row per runner served, and the history budget scales with the live set; R-BTN-87's 50-runner envelope is a target, not a cap.

**Why:** the `rows_read`/day cap is what took production down on 2026-11-14 and 2026-11-15; B-235–B-250 bounded the statements and the per-runner reads, and this is the remainder.

**Done when:** a test seeds transcript and report history past the chosen bound and asserts the view's reads stay at that bound (the same statement capture B-250's tests use), with the served `runners` JSON equal to what the rows inside the bound serve and the rows outside it documented (nulls or exports); EXPLAIN shows no walk the bound cannot pay for.

### Trust

No new input: rows Baton already stores. The bound is the instance's own policy — a constant or a setting, never a request field.

### Invariants

- R-BTN-3: runner rows are never deleted; whatever this prunes is pointers and messages, never a runner row.
- R-BTN-149's retention keeps its own terms — batches, oldest first, through an index, a tick with nothing to delete reading a few rows; this extends its lists on the same terms.
