**Brief:** the owner, 2026-10-20: bindings belong to git, and no owner setting gates them
(`spec/BATON_SPEC_HISTORY.md`, "Decisions in full"). Filed from the ticket plan for the
current-state spec (PR #71).
**Spec:** `spec/BATON_SPEC.md` §7.2 R-BTN-161; R-BTN-160's dispositions and closing table (§7.1);
R-BTN-74 (§4.2, the source's `file(ticket)` export); R-BTN-110 and R-BTN-111 (§5.5, the `std:`
library and the signature); R-BTN-133 and R-BTN-143 (§8.4, the verified home tree, default-branch
code only); R-BTN-86 (§4.6, children) and R-BTN-149 (§2.3, four generations); R-BTN-66 (§1, who may

**Depends on:**

- R3-1258 (child tickets, which handler tickets are).

### Requires

None: a checkout and a terminal.

### Why

Until bindings exist, every condition reaches the debugger. A binding lets a project choose how
it is reached, through a course of its own or a standard one (B-66), and Baton stays out of
delivery.

### What to build

1. **Where bindings are written.**
   - Repository scope: `"conditions": {<type prefix>: <course id>}` in `.baton/baton.json`,
     validated with the rest of that file.
   - Ticket scope: a ticket file's `conditions:`, which `src/defaults/tickets-markdown.ts` reads
     and the sync posts as `tickets[].conditions`. The board post validator (`src/core/board.ts`)
     accepts and bounds it. Baton never resolves a leg's raise from it; B-64 stores it in
     `condition_bindings`, for core's own handlers only.
2. **Resolution, in the runner** (`src/runner/`):
   - Read bindings from the verified home tree at the claim's commit: `.baton/baton.json`, and the
     ticket chain through the source's `tickets` call on B-42's tree. Never read them from Baton's
     rows.
   - Walk from the raising ticket, through its parent, to the root, then the repository.
   - Within a scope the longest matching prefix wins, and the first scope with a match wins.
   - No match means the condition is unhandled.
   - The resolved course and scope go into the handler ticket and the `condition` activity.
3. **Diagnostics.** `baton leg why` and `baton course test --bindings` print, for each type a
   course may raise (B-55's signature), the course it resolves to and the scope that supplied it.
4. **The source's `file(ticket)` export** (R-BTN-74).
   - The markdown source writes `<items>/<local id>.md`, and refuses an existing file that is not
     its own write.
   - B-62 (call children) and B-64 (outbox writes) reuse it.
5. **The handler ticket.** For a bound raise, in this order:
   1. B-55's check.
   2. `POST …/conditions` with `handler`; Baton answers `handler_id`, the raising ticket's local
      id, then `.c<n>`, where `n` is Baton's count of conditions raised on that ticket so far
      (`R3-1205.c3`).
   3. `file(ticket)` with frontmatter `course`, `parent` (the raising ticket), `handles: {type,
      disposition, restarts, default?, severity?, wait_s?, condition_id}` and `raised_by: {leg,
      run}`. The body is the leg's subject and body.
   4. Push, then return.

   The handler is a child, so R-BTN-149's four-generation bound applies. From then on it is an
   ordinary ticket.
6. **Contracts** (`src/shared/std.mjs`, which is not vendored):
   - `std:condition@1`: `{type, disposition, restarts, default?, severity?, raised_by: {ticket,
     leg?, run?}, question: <std:git.file@1>}`.
   - `std:restart@1`: `{restart: <id>|null, reason: chosen|unanswered|withdrawn|resolved,
     answered_by?, commit?}` (its own schema; it does not refine `std:decision@1`).
   - `baton course test` refuses a `suspend` binding whose course does not produce
     `std:restart@1` at every end station. What a `notify` or `interrupt` handler produces is not
     read.
7. **Bound dispositions.**
   - Bound `interrupt` files the handler, then ends as unbound does. Nothing waits for it.
   - Bound `notify` files the handler, and the raise returns at once.
   - Both close when their handler ticket closes. Close them in the transaction that shows the
     handler closed (snapshot or end), using the by-handler index.
   - Bound `suspend` files the handler too. B-59 builds the wait, and until then B-55's
     refusal stands.
8. **Delivery runners** need no core change. A handler course declares its capability types
   (R-BTN-121).
9. **The adversarial review's fixes** (2026-10-20):
   - **Long and reserved ids** (R-BTN-161, adv-2 m2). When `<local>.c<n>` would break the
     ticket-id rule's 64 characters, Baton answers `handler_id` `c<condition_id>`. Ids ending
     `.c<n>` or of the form `c<n>` are reserved: `src/defaults/tickets-markdown.ts` skips a file
     carrying one without a `handles` block, with a sync warning naming it.
   - **No foreign overwrite** (R-BTN-161, adv-2 m2). When `file(ticket)` finds a file at the
     handler's path that is not its own write, the runner writes nothing, fails the raise and
     ends `released:cannot-finish:spec`. The outbox's `outbox-collision` hold for core's handlers
     is B-64's.
   - **`replaces: "retry"`** (R-BTN-161, R-BTN-122, adv-2 m5). An `error` type's binding may be
     `{"course": <id>, "replaces": "retry"}` in either scope: `.baton/baton.json`, the ticket's
     `conditions:` and the board validator (`src/core/board.ts`) accept it, and a non-`error`
     prefix carrying it is refused. Without `replaces`, the handler is filed beside the retry
     policy, which still routes the ticket. With it, the handler's restart (`retry`,
     `route:<leg>` or `park`) decides in the policy's place, and the leg ends blocked on the
     condition as a `suspend` would. That end is B-59's wait and the restart's check against the
     owners' bounds is B-11's: whichever of B-58, B-59 and B-11 lands last wires them, and until
     then a `replaces` binding is refused by `baton course test --bindings`, naming the missing
     piece.

### Do not

- Add an owner setting, or anything in Baton, that gates or overrides a binding.
- Resolve from an unmerged branch (R-BTN-143). An unmerged binding binds nothing.
- Return from a raise, or end the leg, before the handler file is pushed (G-BTN-146).
- Write a core condition's handler here. Those go through B-64's outbox.
