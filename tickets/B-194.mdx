**Brief:** the owner, 2026-11-09 (B-191): "**Board** `/board` — the repository as it stands:
ready tickets in the matching order, live claims and their holders, granted capabilities.
**Tickets** `/tickets` → `/tickets/{t}` — the work itself. A ticket page gathers every read
about it: position (station, path, queued leg), why it can't move (blocks, prerequisites),
history with input/output pointers, children, leg runs with transcripts and log dirs. Its
controls live here too: hold/expedite/cancel, route, adopt, resolve, stop." The mockup's Tickets
table (state column with its shape marks, the reason band under a row — Held by …, Retries
exhausted, Decision open, No capable worker, Waiting on —, course position with holder and
duration, requires as chips, the Ready/Running/Blocked/Unconfirmed/Merged-today filters and the
tier ceiling) is the Tickets screen; its Workers cards' claim block (leg from → to, running for,
lease reaps at with its bar, attempt n of m with backoff, output, earlier attempts) is the ticket
page's claim section.

### What to build

The two §14 rows first, then the code.

1. **`GET /v1/repositories/{repository}/tickets`** (`repo.tickets`, read; callers the repository
   reads' principals and a session): every live ticket's filter fields — `ticket_id`, `title`,
   `course`, `tier`, `requires`, `tags`, `order`, `parent` — with its `position` (station, path
   length, queued leg, R-BTN-83), its `state` (one of `ready`, `running`, `blocked`, `held`,
   `unconfirmed`, `suspended`, `backing-off`, `waiting-person`, `closed`), the `reason` the
   state carries (the holder and since when; the hold's `set_by` and reason; the first block's
   kind and ref and `blocked_since`; the retry count against `max_runs`; the open condition's
   type; the completion reported at), and `closed_at` for tickets closed in the last 24 hours;
   `?state=`, `?q=` (a substring of id or title), `?tier=` and `?cursor=`, 100 a page. Built from
   the rows `readyRows`, `claims.running`, `blocks.view` and `claims.needsYou` already read.
2. **`GET /v1/repositories/{repository}/tickets/{ticket}`** (`ticket.show`, read; same callers):
   one ticket's row as above plus its live claim (holder, hostname, session name, claimed at,
   lease ends, deadline, run id, attempt n of `max_runs` with the next backoff, `session_url`,
   `leg_log.dir` as text), its overrides (expedite, hold with reason and `set_by`), its open
   conditions (ids and types), its parent and the ids of its children, and the resume record's
   branches and PRs (R-BTN-24, as `history` returns them).
3. **The Board** (`/repositories/{r}/board`): from `GET …/board` and `GET …/blocked`: the ready
   list in the matching order (each with requires, tier, eligible alive runners by name, and
   "nobody can take it" with the failed rule — the retired Ready view's content), the live
   claims as holders (ticket, runner, leg, running for, lease ends, stalled when
   `last_activity_at` is over 2 hours old, `condition-wait` — the retired Running view's), the
   repository's granted capabilities, and `ready_by_tier`. The mockup's Overview page content
   that belongs to a repository — the shadow/cut-over banner, the head line — sits at the top.
4. **Tickets** (`/repositories/{r}/tickets`): the mockup's table from `repo.tickets`: ticket
   (qualified id, title linking to its page), state with its shape mark and the reason band, course
   position, requires as chips (a capability no alive runner holds marked as the mockup does);
   the filter tabs with counts, the text filter and the tier ceiling as URL query parameters so
   a filtered view is linkable; a real `<table>`, sortable by the columns the read orders by.
5. **A ticket** (`/repositories/{r}/tickets/{t}`): sections, each from its read, each
   independently polled: position (station, path as stations and legs run, queued leg and its
   source: the course, a route, a decision), the claim (the mockup's claim block, with earlier
   attempts and their ends), why it cannot move (`blocks` and `prerequisites` with `met`, `why`,
   `since`, `until`, a `url` where one passed §12.3), history (`history` paged, each row's kind,
   leg, run, reached, next leg, and its inputs and outputs as pointers — URIs drawn as links
   only when `https://`, digests as text), children (`children` with station and blocked),
   leg runs (`legs/{leg}/runs` per leg: run, runner, mode, started, ended, end, session link
   under R-BTN-113, transcripts as repository paths, `log_dir` as text), and the controls the
   shell places here by `ui.view`: hold, unhold, expedite, cancel, route (leg or clear), adopt
   (at, next, ran), resolve (restart, condition), stop; for an administrator's session force-reap,
   fence clear, the completion's confirm and release — each through the shell's dialogs, a
   `202` through the confirm-code dialog.
6. **Phone.** Tickets and the ticket page read at 375 px: the table collapses to one card per
   row with the state first; the ticket page's sections stack.

### What not to do

- No new write: every control is an existing registry action; the ticket page places them.
- No ticket body: the title is the one text the ticket source posts (R-BTN-33); the page links
  the ticket's own page on its forge by `web_base` (R-BTN-92) for the rest.
- No state computed in the app: `state` and `reason` come from the read; the app never derives
  "blocked" from a claim's absence.
- No course names in code: station, leg and tag names are data (the brief).

### Trust

- **The two new reads** — Baton's own rows, answered to the repository reads' principals and a
  session of its repositories; every text in them (titles, hostnames, reasons, hold reasons,
  condition types, URIs) is untrusted text validated on its write (§12.3) and drawn as a text
  node; a URI is a link only when `https://` and already checked.
- **The controls** — registry actions through `authorize`; the page sends what the form holds
  and shows the refusal's code as text.
- **Query parameters** — `state`, `q`, `tier`, `cursor` validated on the server
  (`invalid-params`); `q` is matched, never interpreted.

### Invariants

- **Positions are Baton's** (R-BTN-39): the page shows the course history; no read or control
  here places a ticket except `ticket.adopt` through its own gate.
- **Filter fields only** (R-BTN-33).
- **Text, never markup** (§12.3); **log directories as text** (R-BTN-128); **session links only
  from `session_hosts`** (R-BTN-113).
- **The confirmed-action list is R-BTN-155's**: unhold, expedite, the completion's confirm and
  release, force-reap and the fence clear go through the code dialog; hold, cancel, stop,
  resolve and route do not.
- **An owner's session, its repositories** (R-BTN-155).

### Tests

Scenarios numbered at merge, on both hosts, with a fixture repository holding a ready, a
running, a held, a blocked, an unconfirmed and a suspended ticket:

- `repo.tickets` returns each with the expected `state` and `reason`; `?state=blocked` and
  `?q=` filter; pages chain by cursor;
- `ticket.show` carries the claim, attempts, overrides, conditions, children and resume;
- the Board's ready order equals `GET …/board`'s and names eligible runners; a ticket nobody
  can take names the failed rule;
- under `test/ui/`: the ticket page draws an owner's session hold, cancel, stop and route and
  not force-reap, an administrator's force-reap too (B-191's scenario, this screen); a hostile
  title and hold reason reach the DOM as text; `log_dir` is text, a `session_url` on an
  unlisted host is text.
